01 Ordering & delivery platform

Al Qaysar Restaurant

Built from scratch Halal Middle Eastern · Manila

Al Qaysar has served halal Middle Eastern food in Manila since 2015, and until this build there was no way to order from them online. The brief was never a website with a menu on it. It was the machinery to take an order, take payment for it, and get a courier to the customer's door — and to do that without anyone in the restaurant having to reconcile the three by hand.

The whole thing runs as one Next.js application. The menu is modelled in Prisma across more than twenty-four categories, with sorting by price, newest and popularity, a daily deals section, and favourites for customers who sign in. Cart and checkout sit on top of that, and checkout hands off to an integrated payment gateway API.

The part that actually needed care is the seam between paying and delivering. Payment state and order state are modelled separately, so an order can only move toward dispatch once the gateway confirms the money has cleared — there is no path through the system where food goes out against an unpaid order. Once an order is confirmed it is handed to Lalamove through their API, which books the courier and owns the delivery leg from there.

Table reservations run alongside all of it on their own data model. A dine-in booking and a delivery order share nothing but a customer, and keeping them separate means neither flow can corrupt the other.

  • Checkout wired to a payment gateway API
  • Lalamove API integration for courier dispatch
  • 24+ category menu with sorting and daily deals
  • Customer accounts, favourites and table reservations
  • 24+ Menu categories
  • 2 Third-party APIs

02 Booking & promoter platform

LOBO Superclub & VIP JTV

Built from scratch Nightlife · Malate, Manila

LOBO Superclub & VIP JTV is a premium nightlife venue in Malate that trades from 8PM to 8AM. Nearly all of its revenue arrives as a table reservation, which meant the site could not simply advertise the venue — it had to be the system the venue is operated from.

What ships as one domain is really three applications over a shared data model. Guests browse VIP packages, the bar area and the events calendar, and reserve a table. Staff work from an admin dashboard where every reservation lands and can be managed, alongside packages, events and site content — this is the part guests never see and the part the venue actually runs its night from. And promoters, the partners who bring people through the door, get accounts of their own.

The promoter programme is what makes this more than a booking form. A promoter registers, invites guests, and earns commission of up to 12% on the business they bring in. That only works if the chain holds end to end: promoter → invited guest → reservation → commission. Every link is attributed in the data, so a payout is calculated from real records rather than counted off receipts at the end of the week.

Underneath sits role-based access across three account types. A guest, a promoter and an admin each reach a different surface of the same application and see only what they should — which is a permissions problem before it is a layout problem.

  • Admin dashboard over every reservation, package and event
  • Promoter accounts with guest invitations and commission tracking
  • Role-based access across guest, promoter and admin
  • VIP table booking, events calendar and venue gallery
  • 3 Account types
  • 12% Max promoter commission

02.2 Process

How I work

The same four steps on every project, in this order. Most of the mistakes I have had to unpick came from skipping the second one.

  1. 01

    Understand the business

    Before any code: what does this place actually make money from? For a restaurant it is completed paid orders; for a club it is booked tables. Whatever that is becomes the thing the system is built around, and everything else is arranged to serve it.

  2. 02

    Model the data

    The schema comes before the first screen. I map the entities and the states they can be in — an order that is unpaid, paid, dispatched — because the features nobody has asked for yet will be built on these tables, and a wrong shape here is expensive to fix later.

  3. 03

    Build the flows

    Then the application: the paths a customer, a promoter or an admin actually walks. Third-party integrations get wired at this stage, and the transitions between them are where I spend the most care — a courier should never be booked against an order the gateway has not cleared.

  4. 04

    Ship and keep it running

    Deploy to Vercel, then stay with it. Performance, image delivery and technical SEO get tuned after launch, and new requirements arrive constantly. Two of the four live sites I work on were inherited from someone else — I know what maintainable looks like from the receiving end.

The restaurant had been trading since 2015 with no way to take orders online. The brief was not a menu page — it was a working delivery business: take the order, take the money, get a rider to the door.

The order workflow

  1. Browse the menu 24+ categorised sections — appetisers, grills, mains, pasta, breakfast, beverages — with sorting by price, newest and popularity, a daily deals section, and favourites for signed-in customers.
  2. Build the order Items go into a cart. Customers can create an account, which is also where the free-delivery incentive is applied.
  3. Pay through the gateway Checkout runs through an integrated payment gateway API. The order is only treated as confirmed once payment clears — payment state and order state are modelled separately so the two can never drift.
  4. Dispatch to Lalamove A confirmed order is handed to Lalamove through their API, which books the courier and handles the delivery leg. The site owns the order; Lalamove owns the ride.
  5. Reservations, on their own track Dine-in guests book a table through a separate flow on the same site, with its own data model independent of orders.

Third-party integrations

This is a transactional system, not a menu page. Money moves through it and a third-party courier is dispatched by it — which means real state to manage: payment status, order status, and a handoff between them that has to be right every time.

LOBO Superclub & VIP JTV

Type
Reservation platform with admin dashboard & promoter programme
Client
LOBO Superclub & VIP JTV — 1917 Adriatico St, Malate, Manila
Built at
AP Creative Advertising Corp
Stack
Next.js · Prisma · Role-based auth · Tailwind · Vercel
Live
lobosuperclub.com

A premium superclub and VIP lounge open 8PM–8AM. The entire business runs on table reservations, so the site could not just advertise the venue — it had to be the system that operates it. It is really three applications sharing one data model.

Three systems in one

  1. The guest side Browse VIP packages, the bar area and the events calendar, view the gallery and location, and reserve a table. This is the only part a visitor ever sees — and the smallest part of the build.
  2. The admin dashboard Staff see every reservation as it lands and manage it from there, along with packages, events and site content. This is what the venue actually runs its night from: who is booked, for which table, under which package.
  3. The promoter programme Promoters register for their own accounts, invite guests, and earn commission of up to 12% on the business they bring in. Every invited guest and the booking that results is attributed back to the promoter who brought them, so commission is calculated from real data rather than counted by hand.

What that requires underneath

  • Three account types — guest, promoter and admin — with role-based access, so each only sees and does what it should.
  • A reservation model covering tables, packages, minimum spend, dates and status.
  • An attribution chain: promoter → invited guest → booking → commission. Every link has to hold for the payout to be correct.
  • An admin surface over all of it, built for staff working at speed during service.

The public pages are the smallest part of this build. Underneath sit three account types, a reservation data model, and a commission attribution chain — the kind of thing that has to reconcile correctly because somebody gets paid from it.

Also in the build

  • Events calendar and nightly programming
  • VIP table and bottle package browsing
  • Venue gallery and dedicated bar area page
  • Google Maps integration and WhatsApp support channel
